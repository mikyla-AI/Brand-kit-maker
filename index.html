Welcome — fill in The Ayurvedic Institute (TAI)

This form generates the brand chapter that Claude (and the rest of the team) uses to produce on-brand copy for every campaign.

This form is built on top of the Universal Rules already locked into the master Brand Doc. You don't need to re-document banned words, contrast constructions, or Dr. Lad accuracy rules — those apply to every brand automatically. Your job is to fill in what makes this brand specifically itself.

  1. Switch brand in the dropdown at the top to fill in any of the five accounts. Each brand's data is saved separately in your browser.
  2. Auto-save runs on every change. If you switch laptops, hit "Export JSON" and re-import on the new device to continue.
  3. Required fields are marked with a red asterisk. Optional fields can be left blank if not relevant.
  4. When done, hit "Export Brand Chapter" at the top to generate a populated markdown file ready to drop into the master Brand Doc.
  5. One sitting takes about 90–120 minutes per brand if you have answers ready. The Worked Examples section is the heaviest lift — set aside time.
Why this form is structured this way
Large language models like Claude work best with example-based reasoning, not abstract voice descriptions. Every section asks for concrete examples, ideally with a Do/Don't pair. The harder you push on specificity here, the better the output the team gets when generating copy down the line.
SECTION 1

Brand Identity

0/4

The five-line identity card. This is what every team member should be able to recite in their sleep about this brand.

Document maintenance

Why this is here
Brand docs decay without owners and version history. Filling this once prevents "wait, which version is the latest?" six months from now.
List the triggers that warrant an update. Example: a new platform is added, a campaign meaningfully outperforms, voice drift is identified, the brand's positioning shifts.

Brand identity

Max 15 words. The single sentence that captures what this brand IS. If a colleague asks "what's this brand?" — this is the answer.
Two sentences max. Sentence 1 = the mission (why we exist). Sentence 2 = the north star (the single thing this brand is built to do day-to-day). For most brands these collapse into one paragraph.
Optional. Leave blank if the brand doesn't use one.
Year + one-sentence founding context.
The version a team member could repeat to a journalist or new student. What was happening when this brand started? What problem did it solve? What's still true today?
SECTION 2

Ideal Customer Profile

0/9

The single person this brand exists to serve. Specificity here drives every other section. Vague answers here produce vague copy everywhere else.

Demographic snapshot

Be specific — name the actual jobs. Not "wellness professionals" but "RNs, NDs, massage therapists, yoga teachers, integrative MDs."

Psychographic profile

What does this person already believe about how the body works? What's their relationship to conventional medicine?
This is gold for hooks. Specific modalities they've already explored, and the gap that remains.
Names, podcasts, authors, social accounts. Helps us understand the existing voice landscape they live in.

Pain points, desires, aspirations, objections

In their words, not yours
Use the exact phrasing this person uses when they describe these to themselves. Not your translation. The closer to their actual language, the better the LLM can write hooks that resonate.
The actual hesitations. "It's too expensive" / "I don't have time" / "I'm not ready to leave my job."
The thing that pushes "I'm thinking about it" into "I just enrolled." Often a life event, a frustration peak, or a specific moment of clarity.

Audience Personas

Give them a name. One paragraph. Format: "Persona Name (job/role). The thing that makes this person different from other personas."
SECTION 4

Voice Fingerprint

0/3

How this brand sounds in three sentences. The fingerprint should be specific enough that a writer or LLM can recognize an off-brand sentence immediately.

Sentence 1 = what we sound like. Sentence 2 = what makes us recognizable. Sentence 3 = what we sound like under pressure (when copy is hard to write).
Average sentence length? Paragraph length? Any structural quirks (e.g., often opens with context, often ends with a question, frequently uses semicolons)?
Name 2–3 brands or styles this brand should never be confused with. The negative anchor is more useful to LLMs than positive description.

Tonal benchmarks — published pieces that calibrate the voice

Most useful field for LLMs
Pointing at specific published pieces is the highest-signal way to anchor voice. Claude (or any writer) can compare new copy directly against these exemplars.
URL + brief notes on why it's the benchmark. Example: "Shayla DiTolla, 'The Lived Experience of a 200-Hour Yoga Teacher Training' — depth without over-explanation, intellectual clarity that still feels human."
Short quoted passages from published work that demonstrate voice moves. Format: "[passage] — [what it does well]"
If this brand sits in a portfolio, name partner accounts whose voice elements should be reflected here. Example: AYC's doc points at @drvasantlad and @theayurvedicinstitute as ecosystem benchmarks.
Most brands have at least one published piece that drifted from voice. Document it here as the contrastive example. Format: "[URL or title] — [what it does wrong] — [what a revision would do differently]"

Brand-specific banned

Format: "phrase — why we don't use it." LLMs need the rationale to pattern-match synonyms.
Sentence patterns this brand never uses. Format each as a Do/Don't pair so the LLM sees the failure mode and the corrected version side by side.

Brand-specific required

Disclaimers, attribution lines, source anchors, and any phrase that must always appear in certain content types. Example: "All ASP students study with Dr. Lad" must appear in any program-overview content.

SECTION 6

POV & Tone Spectrum

0/2

Pronoun rules, tense defaults, and where this brand sits across eight standard tone axes.

POV & pronoun rules

Specify: (a) default voice — "we" / "I" / "you" + when to use each, (b) group nouns — "students" / "practitioners" / "members," (c) default tense — present / past / future. Include 1–2 example sentences.

Tone spectrum (drag to position)

How to use these
Drag each slider to where this brand actually sits, not where you wish it sat. The point is to capture the real voice, even if the real voice is more clinical or more warm than the wellness-industry default.

Tone variations by platform

Platform-specific calibration
Voice stays consistent across platforms; tone shifts. Document where the tone differs (or stays the same) so the team isn't guessing.
How does the voice feel on the blog specifically? What's the energy of a great blog post? What does the blog avoid that other platforms might allow?
Ads are interruptive — what changes about voice when you only have 2 seconds to earn attention?
Often the highest-stakes content. What does this brand's website voice sound like at full strength?
SECTION 7

Worked Examples

0/15

The heaviest section. Five examples per copy type, with at least one explicit Do versus Don't pair. This is the primary training data for Claude — the sharper the examples, the sharper the output.

Honest expectation
This section takes the longest. Block out 60–90 minutes for it. Do it last, after the rest of the form is filled in — by then the voice is locked in your head and the examples come faster.

Before / After rewrites of actual published copy

Highest-leverage LLM training data
AYC's brand doc rewrites real program page copy in Section 11. This is the most useful field in the entire form because it shows exactly how off-brand published copy gets corrected. Add as many as you have time for — even 2–3 rewrites is enough to anchor the voice.
Format each entry as: BEFORE: [actual published copy]. PROBLEM: [what it does wrong]. AFTER: [rewritten in brand voice]. Separate entries with a blank line.
SECTION 8

CTA Library

0/5

CTAs are the highest-leverage piece of copy in any campaign. Each brand maintains a library so the team rotates through tested options instead of inventing new ones every campaign.

5–10 trigger-word CTAs with rationale. Format: "Comment STUDY for the prospectus — used for ASP1 lead gen, conversion rate ~12%."
5–10 direct-link CTAs.
For prospectus, application, curriculum overview pages — where the visitor is already considering enrollment.
CTAs that have failed, feel off-brand, or were tested and underperformed.
SECTION 9

Edge Cases

0/2

Universal edge-case rules (Section 7 of the master Brand Doc) already cover difficult topics, crisis communication, and negative comments. This section is for brand-specific overrides only.

Anything that's specific to this brand on top of universal rules. Example: "TAI never discusses fertility content without flagging Dr. Vrinda Devani's clinical authority on the topic."
Who handles negative comments for this brand? Are there comment types that need leadership review before responding?

Platform-specific banned word exceptions

Where banned-list rules shift by platform
AYC's "journey" rule has a platform exception table — banned in blog/email/IG-AYC-voice, allowed in IG quoted student speech. If this brand has any rule that varies by platform, capture it here.
Format: "[banned word/rule] — [where it stays banned] — [where it has an exception and why]"
SECTION 10

Brand-Specific Review Checklist

0/1

Brand-specific checklist items the reviewer runs after the universal checklist. AYC's structure has a separate checklist per platform because the failure modes differ — Blog vs. Email vs. Instagram have different ways to drift.

Items that apply to all content for this brand. Format: each line is one yes/no check. Examples: "Dr. Lad referenced accurately per Section 1.7?" / "Institutional 'we' used throughout, no 'I' statements?" / "ASP1/ASP2 spelled correctly?"

Per-platform pre-submission checklists

Different platforms drift in different ways
AYC's blog checklist focuses on SEO + voice + opening register. The email checklist focuses on subject lines and one clear CTA. The Instagram checklist focuses on opening hook and hashtag discipline. Capture each platform's specific failure modes here.
